# Flow-cytometry-T-cell-analysis-
High-Dimensional Profiling of MV A-Based HIV Vaccine Responses Reveals Dynamic T-Cell Phenotypic Maturation and Establishment of Multifunctional Memory Populations

This project investigates the longitudinal evolution of vaccine-induced CD4+ T-cell responses following administration of an MVA-based HIV vaccine in five non-human primates using high-dimensional single-cell mass cytometry (CyTOF). A total of 40 FCS files spanning baseline, post-prime, and post-boost timepoints were analyzed to capture dynamic immune remodeling across vaccination stages. The dataset included 31 markers covering lineage identity, activation, proliferation, survival, and effector function, enabling comprehensive phenotypic and functional characterization of T-cell states. Data preprocessing was performed using FlowCore with quality-control filtering and arcsinh transformation prior to downstream analysis. UMAP-based manifold learning was used for dimensionality reduction and visualization of immune landscape changes, followed by unsupervised K-means clustering (k=200, validated at k=100) to identify distinct cellular subpopulations. Longitudinal analyses combining boxplots and statistical testing revealed progressive phenotypic maturation of vaccine-responsive CD4+ T cells after prime and boost immunizations, including expansion of activated and proliferative subsets followed by stabilization into multifunctional memory-like populations. Heatmap-driven cluster annotation and volcano plot analyses further identified temporally regulated marker signatures associated with immune activation, survival, and functional diversification, highlighting the establishment of durable multifunctional T-cell memory following MVA-based HIV vaccination.
